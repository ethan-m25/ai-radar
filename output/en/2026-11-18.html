👀 Worth a look

GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol are out

What it is

OpenAI released two new models. GPT-6.2 Astra is the flagship, built for the hardest professional work (slides, spreadsheets, documents, coding, research). GPT-6.2 Sol is the efficient everyday one, and a lot of OpenAI staff use it as their default model. OpenAI says both use fewer output tokens (the unit models use to count what they write, and what you get billed on) to finish a task.

What it means

Your usage limit goes further, and Astra's API got cheaper too. If you use codex, try Sol and see if it's good enough for everyday stuff.

🎯 How to use it

  1. If you already have ChatGPT or codex: look for GPT-6.2 Sol or Astra in the model picker and give them a try (the sources don't say clearly which plans get them)
  2. The Pro 200 plan already has Sol's ultrafast mode (a faster mode)
🔬 Deep dive (tap to open)

API pricing: Astra costs $8 per million input tokens, $0.40 for cached input, and $40 for output. That's 20% cheaper than GPT-6 Astra, and 60% cheaper on the cached part. Sol is priced the same as 6.1 Sol.

The benchmarks (like Agents' Last Exam, where they say it beats Claude Fable 5.2 by 5.5 points at only 5% of the cost) all come from OpenAI itself, so they need independent testing to confirm.

The official post only lists the upsides. Some people online say it works great and others complain, so your experience will vary. Try it on tasks you know well.

ℹ️ FYI

Gemini runs errands for you, free

What it is

Google's Gemini can now handle small to-dos for you, for free. It runs on a new model, Gemini 4 Hydrogen. It can call local businesses for you (Google Duplex), pay at checkout with Google Pay, and you can forward emails to it to deal with. The Gemini and Google Workspace link-up got better too: it can write and send emails, edit Docs and Sheets, and add several calendar events at once.

What it means

It's a free helper for everyday chores. If you're in one of the six countries, give it a go, but a lot of the new stuff launches in the US first.

🎯 How to use it

  1. Only for users in six countries: the US, Japan, South Korea, Canada, Australia and India
  2. New features like phone calls, payments and forwarding emails roll out in the US first; the sources don't say where to find them or which plans include them
🔬 Deep dive (tap to open)

So far there's just one tweet from Josh Woodward, Google's product lead, so details are thin. Usage limits, whether you need a paid plan, and when other countries get it are all unclear.

Having an AI make calls, pay, or log into sites costs more if it messes up than a chat does. Start with small stuff and watch the payment confirmations.
