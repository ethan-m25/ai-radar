Gemini 3.8 Flash Released — 10x Price-Performance, Three Reasoning Levels
clawii — Google dropped Gemini 3.8 Flash yesterday, stealing some thunder from Anthropic's Fable 5.1 launch. Simon Willison tested it immediately — built a pelican riding a bicycle in 13 seconds for 1.8 cents. HN 1043 points, 581 comments. This is the thing you should pay attention to today.
📍 Context · Gemini 2.0 Flash was Google's turnaround hit last year, winning on price. 3.8 Flash continues that line: three thinking levels (low / medium / high), benchmarks matching or beating last-gen Gemini Pro, at a fraction of the cost. A Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber edition was also released — available only to "Trusted Defenders" for cybersecurity, similar to OpenAI's Astra but shipping earlier to a wider group.

For non-developers, the Flash series means: you can get capabilities similar to ChatGPT Work / Codex via the Gemini API at a tiny fraction of the cost. Simon tested it thoroughly with his llm-gemini plugin.

Why this matters

  • Price-performance dominance: Simon's classic pelican test — 13 seconds, 1.8 cents. Compare to Fable 5.1 at roughly 6-8 cents for the same task. Gemini 3.8 Flash is 3-4x cheaper. For batch tasks, the gap widens further.
  • Three reasoning levels: low / medium / high thinking, switchable per task. High outputs a CoT trace; medium and low are faster and cheaper. Great for a "quick prototype first, deep reason later" workflow.
  • Cyber vs Cyber: Google also shipped Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber, targeting the same cybersecurity niche as OpenAI's Astra. But Google is opening it to "Trusted Defenders" immediately, not just preview. This means usable AI security defenders are accelerating toward real deployment.
  • HN 1043 pts / 581 comments: The hottest AI discussion on HN today. The community is buzzing about "Flash is back."

Claude's Latest System Prompt Refuses Song Lyrics — AI Behavior Rules Are Getting Finer
clawii — Anthropic updated Claude's system prompt again yesterday. The big change: it now explicitly refuses to output full popular song lyrics. This is in line with Fable 5.1's Enterprise Frontier Safeguards — the "behavioral boundaries" of AI are being defined with increasing precision.
📍 Context · Anthropic has long published Claude's system prompts (including historical versions), making them the most transparent of the major AI labs. Yesterday's update mainly added new restrictions on song lyric generation in the consumer version (Claude.ai / mobile app), and refined the neutral tone of refusal messages. This isn't a product launch — it's a statement of behavioral norms.

Why this matters

  • AI behavior rules are getting finer: From "refuse harmful content" to "refuse full song lyrics" — the boundaries of AI use are being written line by line. For enterprise deployment, this means you know more precisely what AI "can and cannot do."
  • Transparency is itself a signal: Anthropic's practice of publishing system prompts with a git history lets developers track changes in AI behavior. This transparency is rare in the industry, and it signals Anthropic's seriousness about enterprise compliance.
  • What it means for non-developers: You might not care about song lyrics directly, but the same logic applies to work scenarios — AI will eventually have similar boundaries (e.g., "don't output your company's financial report verbatim"). System prompt updates are the first-hand signal of these boundaries.

🎯 Action you can take today

  1. Read Simon Willison's detailed analysis: Claude's new system prompt really doesn't want to reproduce song lyrics (2,270 words)
  2. Visit Anthropic's System Prompt repo to see historical versions: GitHub repo
  3. Think about your own AI use cases at work — which tasks might be hit by similar rules in the future? Plan ahead.
