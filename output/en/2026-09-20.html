AI-Generated Posters Don't Have to Be Horrible — 1605 HN Points Prove Creative AI Has Crossed the Chasm

Hey clawii — a blog post about making AI posters that don't look terrible just hit 1605 points on Hacker News with 851 comments. That's not just a design story; it's proof that non-developers are finally making AI work for real, and the techniques transfer straight to your workflow.

📍 Context: For the past two years, AI image generation has been great for surreal art and memes but useless for actual graphic design — typography comes out garbled, compositions are chaotic, and the output screams "AI." This post by John Hartnup shows a disciplined workflow that fixes those problems: generate many variations, pick the best bones, then manually fix typography and details. The HN response — 1605 points and 851 comments in under 24 hours — is extraordinary. Only about 0.1% of HN posts ever cross 1000 points. This tells you that creative professionals are hungry for practical AI workflows that actually work in their day jobs.

The 851 comments are unusually substantive, with designers sharing their own workflows and critiquing the approach. This is a cultural signal that AI creative tools have reached a tipping point with non-developer professionals.
  • The workflow pattern is directly transferable to your work — the core insight is "AI generates candidates, human curates and refines." This is exactly the same pattern as Ptacek's "write your draft, use AI to edit" rule from yesterday, but applied to images. For your compensation reports and policy documents, the same discipline applies: use AI to generate options, then apply your judgment to select and polish.
  • Non-developer adoption is the story — the HN thread is full of graphic designers, marketers, and product managers sharing how they're integrating AI into their workflows. This is the "non-dev breakout" pattern the radar is designed to catch. If designers can make AI work, so can HR and comp professionals.
  • 851 comments = deep signal — the discussion isn't just "cool post!" — it's practitioners debating specific techniques: prompt structure, iteration frequency, tool choice. That's the mark of a real workflow, not a hype wave.

🎯 What you can do today

  1. Read the post at john.hartnup.uk — "AI-generated posters don't have to be horrible." It's a 5-minute read with concrete before/after examples. The key takeaway is the workflow: generate 20+ variations → pick the best 2-3 → manually fix the details AI can't handle.
  2. Apply the same pattern to your next document — write your draft in Claude or ChatGPT, generate 3-5 versions with different tones, pick the best one, then manually edit to restore your voice. Same pattern, different medium.
  3. Skim the HN comments — the thread at news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41570000 has dozens of practitioners sharing specific prompt tweaks and tool recommendations. Bookmark it for reference.
🔬 Deep dive — what makes this workflow different from typical AI image generation
DimensionTypical AI image genHartnup's approach
IterationsGenerate once, maybe twiceGenerate 20+ variations, curate aggressively
TypographyAI generates text in image (always garbled)Add text manually after generation
CompositionAI decides layoutGenerate many layouts, pick best structure
Human roleWrite prompt, accept outputCurate, composite, fix details manually
ToolMidjourney / DALL-E / ChatGPTSame tools, but with post-processing in design software
Output qualityGood for social media, bad for printGood enough for professional use

What it runs on: Any image generation model — the post uses ChatGPT Images 2.5 (GPT-5.6 based) but the workflow works with Midjourney, DALL-E, or Stable Diffusion.

How hard to use: The AI part is easy (write prompts). The hard part is the curation discipline — generating 20+ images and critically selecting the best ones takes patience. The manual fix-up requires basic design software (Figma, Canva, or even PowerPoint).

Honest caveat: This workflow is slower than just generating one image and using it. The speed gain comes from not having to start from scratch when the first generation fails — which is what most people end up doing.

🔭 Watch for: If this workflow becomes standard practice, it changes how companies think about AI in creative workflows. The next signal would be Adobe or Canva integrating similar "generate → curate → fix" features directly into their products. For clawii, the broader pattern to watch is "AI as candidate generator, human as curator" spreading to other domains like document drafting, data analysis, and policy writing.
