🏆 The 1 thing that mattered most this week

Claude is moving from "you go ask it" to "it sits where the work happens"

On Wednesday, Claude pulled whiteboards, editable tables and data analysis into the conversation. Its Data feature can turn a set of metrics into a dashboard, or even a narrated video. On Friday it began joining Google Meet as a participant, listening, taking notes, and leaving the summary and action items inside Claude when the call ends. Both were 🚨, and both point the same way: the containers of your work (spreadsheets, reports, meetings) are starting to be taken over by one AI directly.

For you that is concrete. In comp work the slow part is rarely the arithmetic. It's turning numbers into a write-up and turning a meeting into follow-ups, and these two features press on exactly those spots. The Yogurt story is the same signal in miniature: a free, privacy-first open-source meeting notes app got a 👀 on Thursday, and by Friday the official Meetings launch had squeezed its differentiation. Single-purpose tools are being absorbed by the big platforms, fast.

Other items this week poured cold water on it, and they belong in the same frame. Gemini was reported to route Drive files to human reviewers (Tuesday). Lovable-built apps were reported to expose passports and 200,000+ private messages (Sunday). Goldman pulled Claude Code from its bankers (Monday). The deeper AI gets into your workflow, the more the data boundary matters. For someone who touches compensation data daily, the order is: find out where the data goes, then decide how deep to let it in.

If you only do one thing this week: at your next non-sensitive internal meeting, let Claude join Google Meet and watch what it does. In the same breath, ask IT what your company's data terms are for meeting AI.

📈 Trends building this week

Three directions confirmed or accelerated

① Pricier isn't better: default to the cheap tier, upgrade for hard problems

Fable 5.2 took the #1 spot on Tuesday. On Saturday, Garry Tan ran 52 Claude Code tasks and found scores of roughly 82.7% vs 84.6% against Opus 5.5, while Fable cost 2.8x more per solved task. The same week, Gemini 4 Hydrogen lifted the free tier, Meta's Muse Ember pushed the budget tier lower, and Gemini 4 Carbon/Argon prices were announced to double on December 1. The direction is clear: flagships are strong, but everyday work belongs on the cheaper tier. In practice, set Claude Code's default to Opus 5.5 and switch to Fable only for genuinely hard tasks. If you need Carbon, finish those tasks before the price change. Caveat: this was 52 fairly easy tasks, so don't treat it as a final verdict.

② "Decision models" become a category

On Thursday Gemini launched a decision-model API built for yes/no and scoring judgments, and able to look at video and PDFs. smol.ai called it "decision model day," and OpenAI has a comparable product. The same pattern was noted again in Saturday's items. Three players entering at once means this isn't a one-off headline. The potential use in comp work is batch judgment: does this document comply, does this record look anomalous. It still runs through an API, so you'd need an agent to write the call, and the barrier hasn't dropped to "two clicks." Watch next week for a ready-made entry point.

③ Data boundaries: a shared problem for enterprises and vibe coders

Goldman pulling Claude Code (Monday), Gemini's Drive human-review report (Tuesday) and Lovable's leaks (Sunday) are three independent stories with one point: AI tool rollouts usually fail on compliance, audits and permissions, not capability. Peter Yang's Sunday tip, having Claude audit your own setup, shows self-checking is becoming a habit too. If any small tool you've built has ever held comp data, it's worth going back and checking who can open it.

💀 What I called wrong / overhyped this week

3 demotions: a 🚨 pitched a tier too high, heat without a door, and a tool outrun by the platform

Fable 5.2 🚨: "must see" was one tier too strong. On Tuesday I flagged Fable 5.2's #1 ranking as 🚨 and suggested you try it for a day. Saturday's test says that for everyday coding its edge over Opus 5.5 is small while the cost is 2.8x, and the max setting can burn tokens without delivering. It's still a strong model, but for how you work it should have been 👀. Lesson: a leaderboard win is not the same as a better deal for your workflow.

Local big models: heat, but no way in. Monday's "284B DeepSeek in 64G of RAM" got a 👀, and Friday's Edge0-145B made it in as ℹ️. Both share a problem: either it will likely be very slow, or 145B is too much for an ordinary computer. I never found an entry a non-developer could use in two clicks, and Saturday's llama.cpp v1.0.0 hit the same wall. The whole line should be down-ranked to "right direction, not usable for you yet." High HN scores don't mean your Mac mini can run it.

Yogurt open-source meeting notes: a Thursday 👀, outpaced by Friday. I gave it a 👀 for being free and privacy-first. The next day Claude's official Meetings arrived and squeezed the open-source option's differentiation. Not a total miss, since self-hosted privacy still has value, but I underestimated how fast a big platform can fill a gap. It drops to ℹ️ unless you specifically need data to stay inside the company.

Still tracking correctly: Claude moving into the workplace (two 🚨 confirmed by Wednesday and Friday), cost-aware model choice (confirmed by Saturday's test and the Hydrogen/pricing news), and data-boundary risk (confirmed three times).
