Jev "Decision Model": A New LLM Kind That Outputs Scores, Not Words

Check this out — LLMs just got a new shape. Instead of generating sentences, Jev spits out a score, a category, and a confidence value. Simon Willison wrote 889 words about it yesterday. You want to know this name.

📍 Context: The classic LLM contract is "text in → text out." If you want a score or a classification, you need the model to write numbers inside sentences, then you parse them out. Last week, TypeSafe AI released Jev, proposing a new model category they call "System One" (Simon Willison suggests "Decision Models" is a better name): text input still, but output is floating-point numbers + confidence scores — not sentences at all. Simon Willison wrote an 889-word deep-dive yesterday (Sep 21), a rare long-form endorsement for a genuinely new category.

Why This Matters

  • Brand new output paradigm: Traditional models answer "should I interview this candidate?" by writing a paragraph. Jev gives you a score + confidence directly. No wasted tokens, no parsing.
  • What it means for a non-developer: You do performance ratings, candidate ranking, compliance checks — all day. These are exactly the jobs "decision models" are built for. Soon these tasks might not be "have the AI write something" but "have the AI give a number."
  • A name you might want to remember: "System One / Decision Model" — like "agentic" or "MCP," this term may be everywhere next year. Knowing it now puts you ahead of the Chinese-language news cycle.
  • Strong KOL signal: Simon Willison's 889-word standalone post is his "new capability signal" level endorsement, matching the weight he gave to agentic patterns and MCP when they first emerged.

🎯 What You Can Do Today

  1. Read Simon Willison's post: "Jev introduces a new shape of LLM—System One, aka Decision Models" — 889 words, under 20 minutes.
  2. List the tasks in your day that are "scoring / ranking / classifying" — performance tiers, candidate fit, compliance flags. These are the future beachheads for decision models.
  3. Add "Decision Model" to your radar vocabulary. If you see it repeat in the coming weeks, it's a real trend — not just a one-off launch.

What it runs on: Jev is the first model from TypeSafe AI's "System One" family. It still accepts text input, but internally it skips the step of encoding reasoning results into tokens — outputting structured floats and confidence values directly. This decouples "reasoning" from "expression."

vs what you already know: Right now you ask Claude/GPT to score something by having it "write a number in text," then you regex-parse the response. Jev gives you structured output natively — less overhead, smaller response, cheaper, more reliable. Maggie Appleton (quoted in Willison's post) says "decision models" is a better name than "System One" — meaning the community is still figuring out what to call it.

How hard to set up: Very early. Jev is a new product from TypeSafe AI. The API and hosting model are still being disclosed. No independent benchmarks or third-party tests exist. Short term, you can only read the docs.

Honest caveat: This is a "category-defining" early event, not a "ship-and-use" product. Risk: it could be marketing hype with shallow actual capability. Simon Willison himself is introducing the architecture, not saying "I tested it and it crushes." File the name, don't plug it into production yet.

⚠️ Note: Very few public materials exist for this new category. Don't treat it as a drop-in replacement for your scoring systems tomorrow.
🔭 Next thing to watch: If in the next 1-2 weeks we see: ① independent benchmarks of Jev (especially whether confidence calibration is any good); ② a major player announcing a "decision model" product; ③ more KOLs discussing the category — any one of those upgrades this from "file the name" to "pay attention."
Jargon decoder:
Confidence / Confidence score = How sure the model is about its own judgment, e.g., "This candidate is A-grade, but I'm only 70% confident." For a non-developer, this is more useful than just a score — you know whether to trust it or not.
System One = Borrowed from psychologist Kahneman's "fast thinking." TypeSafe AI uses it to describe a model category that skips verbalizing and goes straight to conclusions.
