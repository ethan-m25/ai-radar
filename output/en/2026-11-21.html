Inkling 2 Mini: a new model you can run on your own machine

What it is

Inkling 2 Mini is a new small model from Thinking Machines (the AI company started by Mira Murati, former OpenAI CTO). The weights are public, so you can download it and run it on your own computer. According to the Artificial Analysis review page, it scores 20 on their intelligence index, and it uses a "looped Transformer" architecture. I couldn't confirm the 26B parameter count from an official source, even though the draft mentions it.

What it means

One more option if you want to run models on your own computer. It doesn't change much for your day-to-day coding with Claude Code and codex.

🎯 How to use it

  1. If you want to try it, search Hugging Face for Inkling 2 Mini and look for the GGUF version from Unsloth (a compressed format that's common for running models locally)
  2. Load it with a free app like LM Studio or Ollama. Check the parameter count and memory requirements first, then decide if you want to download it
🔬 Deep dive (tap to open)

The same company's Inkling 2 Small is a big 594B-parameter model. Right now it's only available on their own Tinker platform, and the weights aren't public. Mini is the smaller one you can actually download.

Small local models usually aren't as good as cloud flagships like Claude or Codex, so they fit best for privacy-sensitive or offline use. The draft's "26B" and "runs on a normal computer" haven't been confirmed officially, so check the model page before you download.

Controlling Neon from your phone with Antigravity

What it is

Antigravity is Google's AI coding tool, and its Remote Control feature lets you control a session running on your computer from any browser. Peter Yang's little trick: open antigravity.google.com on your phone, tap share, and pick "Add to Home Screen". Then it works like an app, and you can chat with all your Antigravity threads from your phone.

What it means

If you want to remote-control AI coding tasks on your computer from your phone without installing an app, this is a way to do it. You mostly use Claude Code and codex, though, so you may not need it.

🎯 How to use it

  1. Open antigravity.google.com in your phone browser, tap "See more" and the share button, then pick "Add to Home Screen"
  2. On your computer, you first need to turn on Remote Control in the Antigravity 2.0 settings
🔬 Deep dive (tap to open)

Google's official docs say Remote Control has to be turned on in the Antigravity 2.0 settings on your computer. After that you can control sessions from a browser and get push notifications when tasks finish.

"Works better than Claude's remote control" is just Peter Yang's personal opinion. Antigravity doesn't have a phone app right now. The Gemini 4 Neon model in Antigravity is open to paid Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers.
