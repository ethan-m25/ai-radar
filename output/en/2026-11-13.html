Claude joins the meeting room: automatic notes

Claude can join a Google Meet as a participant, listen and take notes as it goes, and when the call ends the notes and action items stay in Claude.

Why it matters

  • No more scribbling in meetings — notes and to-dos are ready when the call ends
  • Notes land right in Claude, so you can pick up and edit a doc
  • The team can watch what it writes in real time and correct it on the spot

🎯 What you can do

  1. Connect Google Meet in Claude and try it on your next call
  2. Have it open a doc during a meeting and watch it listen and write
🔬 Deep dive (tap to open)

Different from the meeting tools you use now: Claude actually joins the call as a participant, not just records and transcribes.

Right now it only supports Google Meet; other platforms are said to be coming; the team plan gives the fuller experience.

Honest take: it can get things wrong and needs a human to correct it, so skip it for sensitive meetings for now.

👀 Worth a look

Gemini 4 Hydrogen: a big free-tier upgrade

Google's new cheap, fast model is already in the Gemini app and is now the default engine for AI in Google Search.

Why it matters

  • 5 percentage points more accurate than the last Flash (measured by Box)
  • Same task takes less than half the time, with 12% fewer tokens
  • Free users and Search users get this upgrade automatically

🎯 What you can do

  1. Open the Gemini app, pick Hydrogen and throw a few questions at it
  2. Check whether the AI answers in Google Search got more accurate
🔬 Deep dive (tap to open)

Cheaper and faster than Carbon/Argon — it's the workhorse for everyday chores, not the one you throw hard problems at.

Carbon/Argon prices double after December 1, which is when Hydrogen's cheapness really shows; right now the price gap is small.

Honest take: the free tier still isn't the paid flagship, so don't expect complex analysis from it.
