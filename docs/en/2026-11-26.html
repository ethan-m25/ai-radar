GPT-6.2 limits burn faster

What it is

OpenAI's GPT-6.2 comes in two models: Astra (the flagship) and Sol (the everyday one). At launch the billing was off and most users got an extra discount, so the same work took less of your limit. OpenAI has now fixed it. The same work costs more limit, so you hit your cap faster than last week: 40% faster on Astra and 25% faster on Sol.

What it means

If you run GPT-6.2 in Codex, your limit will drop faster than it did last week. But OpenAI says it still lasts longer than 6 Astra and 6.1 Sol.

🎯 How to use it

  1. Users on the Go, Plus, Pro and Business plans each get one extra "banked reset" (a limit reset). The official tweet doesn't say how it shows up.
  2. If your Codex limit has been running out faster lately, this fix is why. It's not that you changed how you use it.
🔬 Deep dive (tap to open)

OpenAI says other models aren't affected, and they won't charge you back for what you under-paid before.

OpenAI's launch page says GPT-6.2 finishes the same tasks with less output, so even after this price bump your limit may still go further than on the old versions.

The 40% and 25% are compared to last week, not to older models. How long your limit actually lasts depends on how much you use it.

Karpathy: letting Claude run for 7 hours

What it is

Karpathy asked Claude to make a map of the US in the style of the "Civilization" games, filled in with real data. Claude Opus 5.5 downloaded and cleaned up 31 public data sources on its own (census, labor statistics and so on), and spent 7 hours writing 45,000 lines of code. The result is a game-style map you can browse on the web: GDP turns into gold coins, and a city's industries turn into regions.

What it means

Even people who can't code can use AI to make something with a lot of data in it. You do need patience though, and it eats a lot of your limit.

🎯 How to use it

  1. Open https://karpathy.ai/usaciv/ and click around some cities and states to see how the data turns into game language.
  2. If you want to see how it was done, download usaciv-source.zip from the page (MIT open-source license).
🔬 Deep dive (tap to open)

Karpathy used the Claude Code desktop app, with Opus 5.5 on the "medium" setting and ultracode turned on. The Claude Code you use every day can run long tasks like this too.

He said it used up half of his weekly Claude limit, so running something for hours isn't cheap.

Claude decided on its own to skip a few states and territories. The AI put the data together, so not every detail has necessarily been checked. Treat it as a fun demo only.

Astra figures out who made an anonymous picture book in 22 minutes

What it is

History professor Mark Humphries gave GPT-6.2 Astra only a few hints and asked it to find out who made an anonymous 1808 picture book held at Yale's Beinecke Library. Astra searched public historical sources on its own and found a lead on the author in 22 minutes. It also dug up a little-known document in the paper archives, and that document is an important find in itself.

What it means

AI can now do research-style detective work on its own, not just answer questions. It won't change your day-to-day work much, but it's worth knowing it can do this.

🔬 Deep dive (tap to open)

Astra first found a diary entry about a visit from a viscount named Chabot, dated five days before the date the picture book mentions. Then it guessed that his travel diary could prove he drew the book, checked, and it did.

This is the professor's own case report, reposted by Simon Willison. It's not a strict experiment.

Humphries thinks historians will be hit less hard than mathematicians, because writing history depends on human interpretation.
