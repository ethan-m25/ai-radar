📍 Context · On September 6, 2026, OpenAI declared it "RSI day" and published two pieces: a philosophical essay by Chief Scientist Jakub Pachocki titled "An Alien Mind," and a research team's internal measurement report "Research acceleration: The view inside OpenAI." The latter contains a striking chart showing per-researcher AI spend going from ~$20K/year in May to ~$110K/year by late July. The inflection point coincides with internal access to a new model — likely GPT-6 Astra.

Why this matters

  • Internal adoption data is the most honest signal: Lots of people debate whether agents are actually useful. Meanwhile, OpenAI's own researchers are voting with their expense accounts — 5x growth in 2 months, from $20K to $110K annualized per person. That's more convincing than any external benchmark.
  • The Chief Scientist writing about RSI is a big deal: Jakub Pachocki's "An Alien Mind" directly addresses Recursive Self-Improvement, which used to be pure AGI theory. OpenAI now believes agents have brought RSI into practical territory. This suggests they may already be using agents to improve agents.
  • Indirect impact on your vibe coding: If the smartest AI researchers in the world are going all-in on agent workflows, it means agents have crossed from "toy" to "real productivity tool." The same curve applies to Claude Code and Codex — expect more agent tooling to land in the next 1-2 months.

What is RSI? Recursive Self-Improvement means an AI system can improve its own performance by interacting with itself, without human intervention. OpenAI believes agents are already showing RSI capabilities — like an agent improving its own code, or an agent generating better prompts to improve another agent. This essay is the first time the company's leadership has formally discussed RSI.

What is "An Alien Mind" about? Chief Scientist Jakub Pachocki wrote a short essay exploring whether AI systems are developing an "alien mind" — a way of thinking fundamentally different from humans. He argues that RSI will accelerate this and that this is a positive development. The essay is philosophical, but its publication itself is a signal: OpenAI is taking RSI seriously.
